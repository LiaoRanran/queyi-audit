# Code of Conduct

## Our pledge

This is a scientific reproducibility package. We want it to be usable by anyone —
regardless of career stage, institution, nationality, gender, disability, or
whether they agree with the papers. Reviewers, students and practitioners should
be able to open an issue, question a number, or report a defect without being
made to feel unwelcome.

## Expected behaviour

- **Criticise results, not people.** "This number does not trace to the frozen
  matrix" is welcome; "the author is incompetent" is not.
- **Show the reproduction step.** If you claim a number is wrong, say which
  command you ran and what you got. That is the whole point of this package.
- **Assume the honest-error case first.** Every defect in this package so far has
  been a real defect *and* an honest mistake. Both facts matter.
- **Respect anonymity.** Do not attempt to de-anonymise the authors, and do not
  post identity guesses (including in issue titles).

## Unacceptable behaviour

Harassment, personal attacks, discriminatory language, sustained disruption of
discussion, publishing others' private information, and bad-faith attempts to
de-anonymise the authors.

## Scope

This applies to issues, pull requests, discussions hosted alongside this package,
and any private correspondence about it.

## Reporting and enforcement

Report a concern to the package coordinator (the person or address from which you
received this package, or the contact listed in `README.md`). Reports are handled
confidentially. The coordinator may edit or remove comments, close threads, or
refuse further contributions, and will explain the reason publicly when it is safe
to do so.

> **Note on anonymity.** Because this package is distributed for double-blind
> review, enforcement is necessarily light-weight: there is no standing committee
> and no public moderation log. That limitation is stated here rather than papered
> over.
